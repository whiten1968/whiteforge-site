Why this exists

Most chess apps are built for playing. Opening Research Studio is built for studying — understanding an opening properly, grounding every conclusion in real analysis and master practice, and turning that into something you remember at the board.

It brings three things together in one place: curated, sourced research on the openings you play; a live engine and the Masters database to check any position yourself; and your own games, indexed so every position is searchable. What you learn becomes a spaced-repetition deck and, when you want it, a beautifully typeset study booklet.

What it does

  • Opening guides that explain

    Each guide is a complete treatment of an opening — the critical positions, the opponent’s important replies, both sides’ plans, and conclusions backed by engine analysis and model games. You read the explanation beside the board and walk every line move by move, with model games, evidence and source references for the position you’re on.

  • Live analysis

    Turn on Live to analyse the current position. The engine runs locally — Stockfish on the Mac, a built-in engine on iPad — and you choose how many lines and how deep. The Lichess Masters database shows how strong players have continued, and you keep the lines or games worth saving.

  • Your games, searchable by position

    Import your games and every position in them is indexed. Step through a game with a live evaluation, run a full-game review that grades each move, search any position across your whole database, and scout an opponent’s openings and tendencies before you play them.

  • Training that sticks

    Every research guide becomes a spaced-repetition deck — one card per key position, with the move the guide concluded on as the answer. Decks are built from your library automatically; review the positions due, play the move on the board, and the schedule does the rest.

  • Collections

    Group games and positions around a plan, an opponent, or a theme — by reference, without duplicating anything.

  • Print-ready study materials

    Export your work as classically typeset PDFs in the style of a fine chess book: a single-position study sheet, a full repertoire booklet, or a revision workbook to test yourself away from the screen.

  • A real reference database, in a few taps

    Build a broad reference of strong games from The Week in Chess — a freely redistributable collection — with no terminal and no licensing worries. You can also import your Lichess or Chess.com games, any PGN file, or a large existing database on the Mac.

  • iPad and Mac, in sync

    Study at the desk, revise on the sofa. Your games, collections and training stay in step across both devices through your own iCloud account. The heavy lifting — importing and indexing very large databases — stays on the Mac; everything else works on both.

Privacy

Your research is yours. Games, collections and training are stored in your own iCloud account and synced privately between your devices — there are no Whiteforge servers in the data path. The app connects to outside services only when you ask it to: the Lichess and Chess.com public APIs when you import your games, the Lichess Masters database during analysis, and The Week in Chess when you build a reference database. No accounts, no analytics, no tracking. See the full privacy policy for detail.
