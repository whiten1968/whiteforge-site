Getting games into your database

The Games tab holds your Personal Games Database, which analysis, scouting and position search all draw on. The simplest way to fill it with strong master games is to build a reference database: Games sidebar → Import from TWIC… → Build a reference database, then choose a span (last year, last five years, or the full history) and tap Build. It downloads strong games from The Week in Chess in the background and resumes if you quit. Tap the How? button in the Games sidebar for the same guidance inside the app.

Importing your online games

Enter your Lichess or Chess.com username in the Games sidebar and tap Sync. Your games download to your device. Re-running only fetches games you don’t already have; duplicates are skipped.

Importing a PGN file

Import PGN file… adds any .pgn export — from another database program, a tournament, or a friend. Games already in your database are skipped, even from a different source.

Entering a game you played

New game from scoresheet lets you enter the tournament details and the moves on a board, with a live engine evaluation after each move, then saves the game to your database.

Importing a large database (Mac)

If you own a large PGN database (a “megabase”), use Import database (megabase)… on the Mac. It imports millions of games out of process so the app stays responsive, de-duplicates across sources, and builds the search index. Because it can run for a long time it is Mac-only, and it survives quitting — relaunch and it resumes. (Export a SCID base to PGN first if needed.) On iPad, import online games, PGN and TWIC; games you add on the Mac arrive on the iPad over iCloud sync.

The position search index

After adding a lot of games, build the position search index (Games sidebar, under Position Search Index). It makes every position searchable across your games and powers the “in your games” explorer and opponent scouting. Indexing a large database runs in the background and resumes if interrupted.

Research guides

Open a guide from the Openings tab (browse by ECO code or opening name) or from Recently opened on the welcome page. Inside a guide you read the studies beside the board, walk the variation tree move by move, and see model games, engine evidence and sources for the position you’re on, plus the editorial chapters. On iPad a library of guides ships with the app and works offline.

Live analysis

Turn on Live to analyse the current position. The engine runs locally — Stockfish on Mac, a built-in engine on iPad — and you choose the number of lines and the depth. The Masters view shows how strong players have continued. Save the lines or games you want to keep into the guide.

Reviewing your games & scouting

Open any game to step through it with a live evaluation, or run Full review to analyse the whole game at once and grade each move (this runs on iPad too; analysing your whole database at once is a Mac job). Opening insights shows your results by opening, and Scout opponent lets you enter a name to see their openings, tendencies and the lines they rely on.

Training

The Training tab is a spaced-repetition trainer. Each research guide becomes a deck — one card per key position, with the move the guide concluded on as the answer — and decks are built from your library automatically. Review the positions due, play the move on the board, and grade how well you recalled it (Forgot / Hard / Good / Easy). You can also add a single position with Train this position inside a guide.

Sync between devices

Your games, collections and training sync across your iPad and Mac through your iCloud account — no setup beyond being signed into iCloud. Use Sync Now to force a send-and-fetch. If something looks out of date, make sure both devices are signed into the same iCloud account and have a connection, then Sync Now.

Mac vs iPad

Everything — research, analysis, training, collections, importing online games, PGN and TWIC — works on both. The one difference is the very large “megabase” import, which is a Mac job; build on the Mac and the games sync to your iPad.

My data & privacy

Your research, games, collections and training live on your device and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud account — never on Whiteforge servers. Engine analysis runs on your device. See the privacy policy for detail.

Reporting a bug

Email support@whiteforgetech.co.uk with a description of what you were doing, what you expected, and what actually happened. Your device model and OS version help, and so does telling us whether it was on Mac or iPad. We aim to respond within two business days.
