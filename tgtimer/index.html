Built for Watch Enthusiasts and Watchmakers

Rate, beat error & amplitude

Rate shows how many seconds per day the movement gains or loses. Beat error measures how evenly the escapement divides each second between its two half-beats, in milliseconds. Amplitude shows the angular swing of the balance wheel in degrees — a reliable indicator of mainspring condition and lubrication.

Live signal trace & stable readings

A reading builds on screen within a few seconds and keeps refining live as it listens. A live signal trace shows the individual readings settling onto the answer, and it firms up to ‘stable’ once they agree. It never times out — leave it running as long as you like to be certain the reading is rock-steady.

BPH detection, lift angle & 3,700+ presets

Pick your movement from over 3,700 presets and the correct BPH and lift angle are set automatically, giving accurate amplitude without manual configuration. BPH can also be left on Auto-detect when the movement is unknown.

Calibration

A Calibration field on the Setup tab lets you compensate for any systematic offset against a known-rate reference movement.

PDF reports

Tap the share icon to open the report form. Enter the watchmaker name, customer name, brand, movement, serial number and position, then generate a PDF including all readings, the rate-history chart and the analysis count. Share it from the iOS share sheet.

Optional contact microphone

For the strongest signal — especially in a noisy workshop — add a piezo contact microphone via a USB-C audio adapter. TG Timer detects it automatically. Typically under £30, and entirely optional.

Precision Measurements, Explained

On most movements, TG Timer’s rate readings agree with a calibrated bench timegrapher to within 1–2 s/d, beat error to around ±0.1 ms, and amplitude — with the correct movement preset and lift angle — to within roughly ±5°. A Calibration field lets you compensate for any systematic offset against a known-rate reference.

Acoustic measurement depends on a good signal: a quiet room and firm contact between phone and watch. Results vary with movement, condition and environment, and an iPhone will not match a professional bench instrument under all conditions. A side-by-side comparison against a calibrated bench timegrapher is planned.
